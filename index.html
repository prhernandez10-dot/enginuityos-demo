Operating platform for the aviation aftermarket

ERP manages transactions. EnginuityOS governs the decisions.

Buying, repairing and selling aircraft parts and engines is one loop: demand, sourcing, procurement, receiving, inventory, repair, sales, fulfilment, finance, management review. EnginuityOS runs that whole loop for whole assets and for parts packages: it records every decision, makes sure the right person makes it, observes the ERP without ever editing it, and keeps a record nobody can quietly change. AI can advise, but people decide.

Repair can sit before stock (repair, then sell) or after a sale (repair to order, exchange). A teardown turns one asset into many lots. Management review feeds the next round of demand, which is why the stream is a loop and not a line.

The six rules the platform never breaks

  • G1Nothing is overwritten. Every fact is saved as a new record in a chain that shows any tampering.
  • G2When in doubt, refuse. A missing permission or setting stops the action instead of guessing.
  • G3One writer per record. Two people can't silently overwrite each other.
  • G4Replayable. The full state can be rebuilt from the saved records and comes out identical.
  • G5Companies are sealed off. One customer can never see or touch another's data.
  • G6AI never decides. AI can recommend; only an accountable person can approve.

How this demo works

The parts and Data Center walkthroughs run the platform's own code in your browser. The engine walkthrough is a recorded run of the real deal workflow. Nothing is faked, and the things that differ from a server deployment are listed here.

What runs

  • The material ledger: packages, serial-numbered units, purchase-order lines with approval limits, receipts, quality holds and returns, repair orders, quotes and approvals, alert rules that turn into owned tasks, quality acceptance, reservations, sale, the exposure view and the cost recovery review.
  • The lot ledger: lots of consumable material received against a purchase-order receipt, permitted uses, shelf life, allocation to approved work orders, issue, confirmed consumption and returns, with a quantity conservation check.
  • The Daily ERP Data Center: source and dataset registry, mapping versions approved by a second person, content-addressed intake with duplicate and format checks, validation, reconciliation of each day's export against the published state, atomic publication, import history and data freshness. The ERP export is produced by the platform's own seeded synthetic generator.
  • The governed deal workflow (recorded): opportunities, deals, evidence with second-person verification, compliance, approval gates with co-approval, award, custody, settlement, title, the executed outcome with per-asset amounts, plan versus actual, record lock, audit export and replay. The run was recorded by executing the real workflow; the page shows each step's result and the records it left, and the stored records' hash chain is verified live by the same code a server uses.
  • The kernel and the durable journal: the append-only event record, the SHA-256 hash chain and AES-256-GCM encryption of stored details, using the same code a server uses with PostgreSQL.
  • Identity rules: every action is checked against the person's role, their company and whether they are a human, a service or an AI.

What differs from a server

  • Records are kept in memory in your browser by the platform's own in-memory SQL client, the one its tests use, instead of PostgreSQL. The Data Center walkthrough uses the platform's in-memory journal. Reloading the page clears them.
  • Hashing and encryption use audited open-source JavaScript libraries in place of the server's built-in crypto module. The encryption key is a fixed demonstration key.
  • Everyone in the walkthroughs is a role, not a real sign-in. A real deployment signs people in with their company's Microsoft account.
  • The rules and limits come from the platform's synthetic test policy, with purchase approval limits added for this demo company (for example, a $25,000 repair approval limit). A real customer sets its own.
  • Dates in the walkthroughs are fixed so every run gives the same result.

How it fits an existing ERP

  • The ERP stays the system of record. EnginuityOS reads its daily exports through the Data Center and never writes to it, so adopting it does not mean replacing or re-entering anything. The ERP's purchase orders, stock lines, repair orders and sales orders appear here as observations with their source file and row attached.
  • Everything that varies by company is configuration, not code. Roles and who may approve what, approval limits, alert rules and their deadlines, which documents a part needs before it can be sold or issued, and how each ERP export's columns map to the platform's fields are all per-customer settings, versioned and approved by a second person. Change a limit and the next decision is checked against the new one; the old decisions keep the limit they were checked against.
  • The aim is less administration, not more. Alerts are raised by the platform's daily check, not typed by people; a task closes itself when its condition is met; refused attempts are logged without anyone writing them up.

What it does not show

The full operator console, engine valuation, the fleet data service and live connections to a customer's ERP system run on the server and are not part of this page.
